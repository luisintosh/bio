## Design Specificity Verdict

**Design review:** partly authored, and uneven. The ACT graph is the most specific thing on the site: its gold "Human gate" and coral "80%+ regenerate" loop show the trust claim working. The shell around it could belong to any developer, with its mono type, uppercase headings, crop marks, and tilted polaroid. The sky, print marks, polaroid, neon diagram, anime art, and blue résumé band don't form one system.

**Deterministic scan:** the scan found 3 advisory findings in the source and 20 in the browser across three pages.
- **Agreement:** the detector flagged the same 10px role caption as the design review (`src/components/Intro.css:74`). It's the only HTML text under 12px.
- **New from the detector:** stragglers and unbalanced headings on all three pages, and no `text-wrap` rule anywhere in `src`.
- **Easing:** the `bounce-easing` curve is really used 5 times in `src/components/Projects.css`, though only 3 are reported. It's a real but mild overshoot on the wipe and arrow transforms, and the 11 overlay flags on color transitions are effectively false positives.
- **False positive:** `text-occlusion` on the `h1`. Its box overlaps the polaroid, but the text doesn't.
- **Detector gap:** the smallest SVG labels in the ACT graph render at about 10.7px and weren't flagged.

**Clean checks:** there was no horizontal overflow at 1440 or 390 wide, and no console errors or failed requests. All HTML text under 18px has at least 5.02:1 contrast, and reduced motion works in the CSS.

**Visual overlays:** the overlays are visible in the `[Human] home` tab of the agent-browser Chromium window, though it wasn't confirmed that window is in front on the user's screen. It shows 16 flagged elements, and reloading removes them because the servers are now stopped.

## Overall Impression

The site proves the trust claim better than most portfolios, but it makes a recruiter work to confirm the basics. The biggest opportunity is to make role, location, and all three parts of the position readable on the first screen, then extend the "human gate" idea beyond one diagram.

## What's Working

1. **ACT graph and "How I build AI systems":** they show trust through named mechanisms, and the case study continues that with its Decisions section.
2. **Contact block:** the role is restated and the address is selectable beside a large button, which protects webmail users.
3. **Copy discipline:** titles lead with outcomes and limits are honest, as in "The platform is private, so this page covers its architecture."

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The fit scan is scattered, and cloud delivery is nearly absent
- **Why it matters:** the role appears only as a 10px rotated caption, and "Remote from Mexico" appears only in Contact. AWS and GCP sit inside the Education paragraph, so the three-part position reads as AI plus TypeScript. A recruiter has to collect these facts from four places.
- **Fix:** add `{profile.role} · {contact.location}` above the `h1` (`src/components/Intro.tsx:30`). Add the two cloud certifications after the subhead, promote Experience and Education to `.section-title`, and split Education into a list. This uses only existing copy.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`, then `/impeccable typeset`

### [P1] The SoloDev plate works against "trust in production"
- **Why it matters:** the demon-and-neon poster is the loudest image on the page, and it sits on the project about approvals, budgets, and independent review. Its baked-in text is also missing from the alt text. This is a judgment call, since the art is SoloDev's own branding.
- **Fix:** on the home page, use a plate in the ACT graph's language showing approved spec → implementation → fresh-context review → PR, with the budget limits. Keep the art for the case study if wanted.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable quieter`

### [P2] Project rows leave ownership and reasoning behind a click
- **Why it matters:** each desktop row has about 180px of blank space, while the ACT row never says what Luis did. A hiring manager has to open a case study to see any judgment.
- **Fix:** show the three existing decision titles in that space on desktop, and use the existing ownership sentence in the ACT summary. Keep mobile shorter, since projects already take 2,163px of the 5,213px page.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P2] The email action comes only at the end of the page
- **Why it matters:** the button is about 3,520px down on desktop and 4,942px down on mobile, and the nav isn't sticky. "Contact" does jump there in one click, but it doesn't say it means email.
- **Fix:** add a quiet `Email me` link to the hero using the existing `MAILTO`, and rename the nav item to "Email". Swap YouTube for GitHub in the nav, since hiring managers look for GitHub.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P2] The carousel hides evidence and drops keyboard focus
- **Why it matters:** four of five named recommendations sit behind clicks. Reaching the last slide disables Next and sends keyboard focus to `body`, and the live region announces only "2 of 5", not the quote.
- **Fix:** replace it with a static list of all five short quotes. If it stays, use `aria-disabled` and announce the quote.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`, then `/impeccable harden`
